Hackathon Challenge:
"Build a digital product that helps a family caregiver identify, obtain, and pay for the right non-clinical support during the first seventy-two hours after discharge.

The first 72 hours.
An adult returns home after a hospital stay.The hospital has already provided clinical discharge instructions. The family now faces dozens of non-clinical -- and potentially costly -- task. Think: Transportation, Meals, Household assistance, appointment logistics, equipment setup, caregiver relief, family coordination, finding trusted providers.


A couple of lenses (from speaker):
What happens if they're houseless? Care does not end with the discharge. (Rally family members, community members, etc.) What are the gaps that need to be filled after discharge? One of the big ones is transportation. What about food? The caregivers (after leaving the hospital, friends, family, etc.) are probably not trained. Will they thumb through a large discharge packet? Population lens: we want to make sure there aren't barriers to different communities. What about language barriers? Socioeconomic? Etc. How can we leverage modern technology that gets them the care they actually need, and is scalable? Accountability: How can we make sure what we intended to happen, happened? How do we track outcomes? How does payment work for people in the US who are foreign.


Parameters around the build:
What we need to show:
Needs to be simple for the caregiver.
A caregiver completes the journey in under ten minutes.
Mocked payment works. Show who pays, what they pay, and why.
Demo it live at your table. 


Questions asked:
Is there a set budget for the user? "Assume a lot of people are struggling right now, there are a fairly large portion in the medical field that aren't well-off" "There are a lot of resources for people, but finding them is often hard" "Connecting between resources is just has hard as making the resources."

What are some things that are overlooked in a field like this? "The siloing of information is really hard for individuals" "If you can get a patient profile, you can make a solution that is customized to a patient and that is healthcare in a nutshell" "A big thing that is assumed is that everyone is in the same 'playing field'" "Assigning accountability to ensure that it's actually done- did the caregiver actually do what they said they would do, often people come back to my office, didn't do the thing, and now we might need to do another surgery..."

Where have you seen previous digital health products fail? "Usually these digital solutions solve problems that they create themselves, not an actual one." "So many digital companies fail because they aren't taking into account individual people experiences. People are irrational, and that isn't accounted for" " 


How judges score:
1-5 Points: Caregiver problem and value. Does it meet a specific non-clinical need in the first 72 hours and create clear value for the caregiver?
1-5 points: Working product experience. Can a caregiver move from need to fulfillment in under ten minutes? 
1-5 Points: Payment and business-model logic. Who pays, what do they pay, and why?
